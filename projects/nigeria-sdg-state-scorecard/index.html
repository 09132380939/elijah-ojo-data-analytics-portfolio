Key findings

  • A clear north–south divide. Mean composite score: South West 64.6, South East 61.0, South South 55.7, North Central 44.0, North East 29.7, North West 24.2.
  • Child survival is where the gap is widest. The North West’s median under-5 mortality (159 per 1,000) is nearly three times the South East’s (57).
  • Infrastructure and inclusion travel together. States with more electricity also have more internet users (r = 0.73), and primary completion tracks poverty (r = −0.72).
  • Women are behind on access. Average gaps are 16.8 points for internet use and 12.3 points for account ownership, widest in Delta (38 points, internet) and the FCT (30 points, accounts).
  • Safe drinking water is scarce nearly everywhere. Zone medians for safely managed water range from 2% to 17%, and no state exceeds 55%.

Method and caveats

Composite score: each of ten indicators is min–max scaled to 0–1 across states, flipped where lower is better, then averaged with equal weights and multiplied by 100. Indicators come from different surveys and years (2019–2022). Borno has no poverty-line estimate, so its score uses nine indicators. Internet use and account ownership are the average of male and female rates. Zones follow the standard six-zone grouping, with the FCT in North Central. Rankings describe relative position, not absolute performance, and small-sample survey estimates can be noisy at state level.
